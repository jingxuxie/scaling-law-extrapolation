\section{Exact-Risk Experiments}

All experiments use exact zeta-tail risks and run in seconds on a laptop. Code generates every figure and table from a fixed seed.

\paragraph{Matched one-power curves.}
We use $E=0.20$, $S=0.08$, $\alpha=0.35$, $\beta=0.55$, and nine pilot scales with $t\in[-0.5,0.5]$. With per-point standard deviation $0.005$, the exact Gaussian KL divergence between the pilot experiments is $0.028$. At $t_\star=6.5$, the risk predictions differ by $0.0223$ (Figure~\ref{fig:matched}). This illustrates the mechanism in Theorem~\ref{thm:matched}; the theorem itself applies to all parameter choices satisfying its conditions.

\paragraph{Hidden spectral component.}
We set $(\alpha,\beta)=(0.25,0.80)$, use nine pilot sizes from $32$ to $4096$, and choose $B$ according to Theorem~\ref{thm:hidden} with $\sigma=0.002$. The hidden-to-visible excess-risk ratio is $0.043$ at $M=32$, $0.62$ at $4096$, $45.1$ at $10^7$, and $567$ at $10^9$ (Figure~\ref{fig:hidden}). A best single-power fit over the pilot points estimates exponent $0.756$ and has maximum residual only $0.027\sigma$. Nonetheless, it underestimates the model size needed to reach excess risk $3\times10^{-5}$ by $13.5\times$, and the size needed for $10^{-5}$ by $186\times$. Thus an apparently decisive fit can be decision-theoretically wrong.

\paragraph{Certificate calibration.}
We simulated 250 noisy pilot datasets from a two-component curve contained in a six-exponent dictionary and requested $90\%$ simultaneous coverage at four targets from $4096$ to $10^9$. One confidence set was empty, as permitted under the $10\%$ model-rejection probability. Counting an empty set as noncoverage, simultaneous target coverage was $98.8\%$ (247/250); conditional on feasibility it was $99.2\%$ (247/249), reflecting the conservatism of Bonferroni bands. Mean interval width among feasible runs increased from $8.63\times10^{-4}$ at the edge of the pilot range to $3.04\times10^{-3}$ at $10^9$. These experiments validate implementation and coverage, not yet superiority on real neural scaling curves.
