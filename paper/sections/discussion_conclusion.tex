\section{Implications and Limitations}

\paragraph{A scaling claim should name its target.}
Table~\ref{tab:targets} separates exponent, target-risk, and resource identification. A local slope may predict the next measured loss while failing to identify the asymptotic exponent, and a small loss error can imply a large error in the model size needed for a stringent threshold. Reporting the decision functional and extrapolation horizon therefore makes a claim easier to audit.

\paragraph{Scale diversity versus replication.}
Corollary~\ref{cor:design-span} sharpens pilot placement through $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$. Central repeats estimate a mean, while separated log-scales expose curvature that distinguishes the floor from the exponent. Replication remains useful for stochastic uncertainty, but its contribution to the local safe horizon is only $m^{1/4}$. A practical ladder should reserve budget for separated scales near the feasible boundaries and use repeats at fewer informative locations.

\paragraph{Coverage is conditional on visible assumptions.}
No bounded pilot interval can exclude every slower component. \ScaleCert\ requires an exponent range, positivity, observation bands, and any mismatch radius to be declared. A broad class may yield a wide or infinite interval; this is an abstention, not a failure. Conversely, a narrow interval is meaningful only relative to the stated class. The public experiment shows that the minimum in-range residual cannot automatically become a future mismatch guarantee.

\paragraph{Relation to other uncertainty methods.}
Bayesian intervals depend on a curve prior, while residual conformalization needs a calibration regime connected to the target. These methods can tighten stochastic bands; partial identification keeps uncertainty about unobserved spectral structure explicit.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The theory covers monotone positive mixtures and independent Gaussian pilot errors. Nonmonotone transitions, signed cancellations, adaptive pilot selection, and correlated errors require extensions. The OLS bridge is exact for whitened Gaussian design; ridge, early-stopped SGD, and neural networks need further analysis. The public evaluation is descriptive because independent checkpoint standard errors are unavailable. Finally, the lower bounds are worst-case results, not claims that every forecast fails.

\paragraph{Recommended reporting.}
A defensible extrapolation report should state the observed log-span, target horizon, pilot locations or $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$, pilot uncertainty, structural class, and target interval. It should also show sensitivity to a wider exponent range or mismatch radius. This reveals whether confidence comes from data coverage or assumptions.

\section{Conclusion}

High in-range $R^2$ is not a certificate. Hidden spectral mass can alter the eventual exponent and resource need, while floor--exponent confounding creates a span barrier. \ScaleCert\ returns intervals that contract only when the declared assumptions and pilot design exclude relevant alternatives.
